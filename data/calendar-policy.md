# Reading policy and cache contract

## Activation is explicit

The production calendar has **not yet been confirmed**. The default remains
`READING_CALENDAR=vatican-vi`: an accepted Gospel from the exact dated Vietnamese
Vatican News page. This describes a source policy, not Vietnam's national or any
particular parish's Mass calendar.

Calendar namespaces and maintenance availability:

| Namespace | Definitions | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `roman-general` | Romcal General Roman; Epiphany January 6, Ascension/Corpus Christi Thursday | Candidate evaluation, not production approval |
| `vietnam` | Requires reviewed national definitions and their own rules version | Automatic Romcal export/derived cache **blocked** |

The pinned Vietnam plugin places the Vietnamese Martyrs on November 13 and
leaves November 24 as a weekday. `vietnam` manifests/reusable reviews and derived
lookups with automatic `romcal-` rules versions are rejected. No guessed national
overlay replaces it; independently reviewed date decisions remain separate.
See [the evidence and provider investigation](calendar-research.md).

Confirm national/diocesan rules, particular celebrations, default options and
coverage before activation. A diocesan/parish overlay needs its own reviewed
policy and distinct implemented namespace/adapter; neither local adapter is
implemented yet. Do not silently treat it as a national profile. In particular, the
November 15, 2026 **intended local selection** remains unresolved even though the
Roman General temporal Sunday has an independently established Gospel.

Definitions are generated by `tools/calendar`, with exact dependency versions and
lockfile integrity. The current provider is Romcal `3.0.0-dev.144` (MIT), a **beta**
release. It is a secondary implementation, not the authority on the Mass. Its
current calendar definitions can also differ from historical/local rules. The
provider and adapter version must be reviewed when upgraded. Node is required
only for maintenance/export and provider tests, never for Go server/CLI lookups.

Relevant primary norms include:

- [Mysterii Paschalis](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/motu_proprio/documents/hf_p-vi_motu-proprio_19690214_mysterii-paschalis.html), promulgating the Roman calendar and General Norms for the Liturgical Year and Calendar.
- [GIRM chapter IX](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-9): selection of Masses and readings, including memorials and permitted alternatives.
- [General Introduction to the Lectionary §69 and Sunday tables](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Resources/Rites/Lectionary.pdf): weekday Gospels repeat annually; I/II distinguishes the first reading, not the Gospel.
- The applicable approved national/diocesan calendar and lectionary must be
  identified before claiming agreement with that local Mass calendar.

The importer derives entry identities from the selected observance, not the old
seasonal key. Three reviewed invariant proper identities are recognized: Lateran
dedication, All Saints, and Annunciation. Default weekday **Gospel-only** entries
repeat annually without I/II; Sunday entries retain A/B/C. Other proper entries
conservatively retain cycles and, for memorials, temporal context. Manifests must
declare `gospel_entry_schema: 2`; old/missing/unknown entry schemas require
regeneration, not silent approval migration. Optional celebrations are not selected
implicitly. Only the default daytime option `day` is currently supported; vigil,
Christmas night/dawn variants, and other unspecified options are unavailable.
Holy Thursday and Holy Saturday do not receive a guessed generic daytime Mass.

## Evidence is not interchangeable

- `reading_calendar_days`: a selected observance from a declared, versioned
  calendar. Importing definitions does **not** approve a Gospel mapping.
- `reading_observations`: append-only facts from dated Vatican News pages,
  including their actual source dates. They are candidates, not general rules or
  approvals of national-calendar scope.
- `reading_entry_reviews` + `reading_entries`: explicit reviews of a reusable
  entry, its Gospel reference, option, actual evidence URL/date, and applicability.
  A matching/majority legacy reference is never automatically promoted.
- `reading_date_reviews` + `reading_date_acceptances`: explicit acceptance of
  evidence for one date/calendar/option. This does not approve or replace a shared
  entry. It can represent an approved local exception or permitted date choice.
- `daily_reading_sources`: retained compatibility evidence for the source-only
  policy. It is not silently relabeled as evidence for another calendar.

Lookup order for an explicitly selected calendar:

1. A valid date-specific acceptance under its active rules version.
2. Otherwise, its supported calendar day and reviewed reusable entry.
3. Otherwise, unavailable. Never fall back to another calendar or a legacy key.

An existing date-specific decision with invalid/stale evidence blocks fallback;
it cannot silently become an ordinary weekday. Rule changes invalidate dependent
resolutions of that calendar, not unrelated calendars. Review histories remain.
A new conflicting observation of the same Vatican page quarantines the source-only
selection, but cannot automatically poison independently reviewed local selections.

## Output semantics

`verified: true` means the selection has supported evidence, **not** that a new
page was fetched for the requested date. Inspect:

- `resolution_method`: `source_checked` for accepted exact-date evidence, or
  `calendar_resolved` for a reviewed reusable entry.
- `calendar_id`, `mass_option`, `rules_version`, `entry_key`: scope and resolution.
- `source_url`, `evidence_date`: the actual reference evidence, which may be from
  another year when an entry is reused. Never synthesize a target-date citation.
- `verified_at`: acceptance/review time; not a fabricated fresh crawl time.
- `calendar_source_url`: the calendar provider, separately from Gospel evidence.
- `celebration`: a reviewed date label or label of the actual resolved observance;
  never a label inferred from the legacy seasonal key.
- `lectionary_key`: compatibility estimate, marked `lectionary_key_is_estimate`.

Availability also requires complete local verse text for the requested ranges.
Missing text is reported separately; repeatedly crawling the reference cannot
repair the shared verse store. Use the existing verse-crawler/TSV import pipeline
for a reviewed missing-text repair; automatic text ingestion is not added here.
This is a **structural** completeness check (verse rows, explicit suffix endpoints,
known chapter bounds), not a word-level validator. A legacy base-verse row can
itself contain a liturgical fragment. Audit actual passage text against appropriate
sources before certifying new mappings; row counts alone cannot establish a
complete canonical Bible or all long/short variants.

## Safe operator workflow

Always use a temporary, consistent database copy for an audit:

```sh
mkdir -p build
sqlite3 -readonly resources/bible.db ".backup 'build/reading-audit.db'"
export DB_PATH=build/reading-audit.db
export READING_CALENDAR=roman-general # evaluation, NOT a production-policy approval
./.github/scripts/prepare-calendar.sh '2021,2022,2023,2024,2025,2026,2027'
go run -tags "fts5" ./tools/lectionarycrawler \
  -years=2021,2022,2023,2024,2025,2026,2027 -calendar-file=build/calendar.json \
  -offline -audit-report=build/reading-audit.json
```

The report classifies every stored/imported date, retains legacy refs and source
observations, identifies unreviewed entries, missing text, unsupported dates, and
stale date decisions, and makes **zero Gospel HTTP requests**. Inventory success
is not a claim that every date is available. Review the unresolved counts.

The report's `entries` queue groups by actual entry key **and rules version**,
with deterministic date/status lists, normalized legacy candidates, structural
text statuses, dated observations, and accepted evidence. `has_reference_variants`
flags differences for investigation, not proof of an incorrect or majority-approved
reference. Row counts are dates, not distinct reusable entries. For example:

```sh
jq '[.entries[] | select(.has_reference_variants)]' \
  build/reading-audit.json > build/variant-entries.json
```

Queue generation is local/read-only; it neither crawls nor approves candidates.

After reviewing an entry's applicability and reference, explicitly approve it:

```sh
go run -tags "fts5" ./tools/lectionarycrawler \
  -review-entry=2026-11-09 -ref='Ga 2,13-22' \
  -evidence='https://www.vaticannews.va/vi/loi-chua-hang-ngay/2026/11/09.html' \
  -evidence-date=2026-11-09 \
  -review-note='Reviewed invariant Lateran proper Gospel, daytime Mass, all cycles'
```

`-review-entry` is an explicit generalization decision. A source observation
alone is insufficient: confirm scope, formulary, cycles, and allowed form.
For a date-only decision, use `-accept-date=YYYY-MM-DD` with the same evidence flags
and optional `-date-label`; its evidence date must match that exact date.
`-replace-entry` and `-replace-date` are explicit replacement operations retaining
review history. None of these operations rewrites all legacy `gospel_ref` rows.

Use a bounded `-from`/`-to` run to fill actual gaps. With a supported warm cache it
makes no HTTP requests, including for future dates. A missing mapping fetches at
most one observation candidate per actual entry, reusing existing observations on
later runs; it does not approve that candidate. `-refresh` explicitly audits the
requested dates and reports disagreements without replacing reviewed entries.

Year extension is offline. Healing checks the selected policy and targets only
actual gaps. The separate source-audit workflow uses a disposable database and
publishes reports, not reference/data changes.

## Release gate

Do not deploy this as an archive-wide repair until the calendar is confirmed,
entry/date approvals and coverage are reviewed, and both backend and `gospel-site`
understand the same schema/evidence semantics. Set `READING_CALENDAR` consistently
in backend, frontend deployment, and both repositories' workflow variables.
Review remaining unavailable dates rather than silently grandfathering predictions.
Checkpoint WAL data before packaging; retain both rollback snapshots and commits.
